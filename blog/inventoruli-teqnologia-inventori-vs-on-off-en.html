Inverter AC or On/Off: What is the difference between them?

When buying an air conditioner, you will inevitably come across two main terms: Inverter and standard On/Off (non-inverter). At first glance, both do the same job — cool or heat a room, but their working principle, energy consumption, and comfort level differ radically.

Imagine driving a car where you can only adjust the speed in two ways: either you press the gas pedal all the way down, or you let it go completely. The ride is uncomfortable and fuel is wasted. That's exactly how a traditional On/Off AC works.

How does an On/Off (regular) air conditioner work?

A standard On/Off air conditioner has only two operating modes for the compressor: On (at 100% capacity) or Off. When you set the room to 24 degrees, it turns on at full capacity, blows maximum cold air, and when the temperature drops to 24°C — it turns off. After a while, the room warms up again, the sensor detects it, and the compressor turns on again. This continuous on-off cycle causes sharp temperature fluctuations and high electricity consumption.

How does Inverter technology work?

Now imagine a car that can smoothly change speed and maintain it steadily (cruise control). An inverter air conditioner works just like that. It has a variable-speed compressor. When it reaches the desired temperature, it doesn't turn off; instead, it simply reduces the rotation speed and, using minimal energy, steadily maintains the desired climate in the room.

Main Differences: Inverter vs On/Off

  • Energy Efficiency: Inverter models consume 30% to 50% less electricity, as they don't require constant turning on and off (which demands the most energy).
  • Temperature Stability: An inverter maintains the temperature with an accuracy of ±0.5°C, while an On/Off AC causes noticeable temperature fluctuations and airflow strikes create discomfort.
  • Noise Level: Since the inverter compressor mostly runs at low speed, it is much quieter than an On/Off system.
  • Lifespan: Fewer on-off cycles mean less wear and tear on the compressor, hence inverter systems last longer.
  • Initial Price: On/Off air conditioners are relatively cheaper to purchase, but in the long run, the inverter pays for itself through the electricity saved.

Which one should you choose?

If you use the air conditioner frequently, or need it for a bedroom, children's room, or living room where peace, comfort, and a stable temperature are important — an inverter air conditioner is undoubtedly the best choice.

However, if you need an air conditioner for a space where you will rarely be (e.g., a summer house), or the door is constantly open and the air changes frequently (a shop, commercial space), buying a budget-friendly On/Off model might be more justified.
